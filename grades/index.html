The right math practice for your child's grade

From first-grade number sense to fifth-grade fractions and long division — see what kids work on each year, and how Math Magic turns it into an adventure they'll actually ask to play.

🌱 1st Grade · Ages 6–7

Building number sense that sticks

First grade is where math confidence is born. Kids move from counting to really understanding numbers — how they combine, split, and compare.

What kids typically work on

  • Addition and subtraction within 20
  • Counting patterns and comparing numbers
  • Place value — tens and ones
  • Simple word problems
How Math Magic helps

Number-sense adventures let kids see quantities, not just memorize them. Friendly visuals, gentle pacing, and a coach that celebrates every try make the first steps feel like play — because they are.

☀️ 2nd Grade · Ages 7–8

Bigger numbers, stronger strategies

Second graders stretch into larger numbers and start spotting the patterns that make math feel like a puzzle instead of a chore.

What kids typically work on

  • Addition and subtraction within 100
  • Place value to 1,000
  • Skip counting — the runway to multiplication
  • Mental math strategies
How Math Magic helps

Visual practice makes place value click, while streaks, stars, and level-ups keep kids coming back for the daily reps that build real fluency — no flashcard battles at the kitchen table.

🔥 3rd Grade · Ages 8–9

The multiplication year

Third grade is the big one: multiplication, division, and a first look at fractions. It's also where many kids decide whether math is "their thing." We're here to make sure it is.

What kids typically work on

  • Multiplication and division facts
  • Understanding fractions as parts of a whole
  • Multi-step word problems
  • Telling time, measurement, and graphs
How Math Magic helps

Instead of drilling times tables, kids see multiplication as groups, arrays, and jumps — and when they get stuck, the AI coach walks them through the idea step by step, as many times as they need, with zero judgment.

🚀 4th Grade · Ages 9–10

Fractions get real, division gets long

Fourth grade brings the topics most parents remember dreading: equivalent fractions, multi-digit multiplication, and the infamous long division.

What kids typically work on

  • Equivalent fractions and comparing fractions
  • Multi-digit multiplication
  • Long division with remainders
  • Decimals meet fractions
How Math Magic helps

Fraction adventures turn abstract slices into things kids can see and move. Long division becomes a step-by-step quest with a coach at their side — so "I don't get it" turns into "watch this!"

⭐ 5th Grade · Ages 10–11

Putting it all together

Fifth graders combine everything — fractions, decimals, and division — and get ready for middle school math. Confidence here pays off for years.

What kids typically work on

  • Adding, subtracting, and multiplying fractions
  • Decimal operations and place value
  • Long division fluency
  • Multi-step problems that mix it all
How Math Magic helps

Practice adapts to where your child actually is — reinforcing shaky skills and stretching strong ones. The Parent Hub shows you the week's wins and where a little help goes far, in about two minutes.

Parent FAQ

Questions parents ask us

Is Math Magic free?

Yes — you can play instantly in any browser with no signup required, and the Android beta is free to join. There are no ads and no in-app purchases.

What grades and ages is Math Magic for?

Math Magic is designed for ages 6–12 — the heart of elementary school. Practice spans number sense, multiplication and division, fractions, decimals, and long division, roughly 1st through 5th grade and into middle school.

Is Math Magic safe for kids?

Yes. There are no ads, no in-app purchases, and no external links inside the app. We collect only what learning needs — never sold, never used for ads, never shared with advertisers — and we are COPPA-committed.

Can I see my child's progress?

Yes. The free Parent Hub shows a gentle, growth-framed snapshot of your child's week — stars earned, problems solved, and skills in progress — plus an optional weekly email you control.

Does it match what my child learns at school?

Content is designed to complement school learning, homework, and test prep — the same core skills taught in elementary classrooms, practiced visually and at your child's own pace.

How long should my child practice each day?

Sessions are built to be short and satisfying — 10 to 15 minutes a few times a week goes a long way. Streaks and rewards encourage a steady rhythm without pressure.
